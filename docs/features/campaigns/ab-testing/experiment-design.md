# Experiment Design & Statistical Significance

PenguinMails goes beyond simple percentages by calculating the statistical confidence of your test results.

## 1. Multi-Variant Testing

While simple A/B tests are most common, PenguinMails supports up to 5 variants (A/B/C/D/E). This is useful for testing broad ranges of variables, such as 5 different subject lines simultaneously.

## 2. Sample Size Guidelines

To get a reliable result, your test sample must be large enough.

| Total Audience | Recommended Test Sample | Min. Duration |
|----------------|-------------------------|---------------|
| < 1,000 | 50% (Split half/half) | 2 hours |
| 1,000 - 5,000 | 30% | 4 hours |
| 5,000 - 20,000 | 20% | 4 hours |
| 20,000+ | 10% | 6 hours |

## 3. Statistical Significance

We calculate a **Confidence Score** for every test. A score of 95% or higher indicates that the winning variant's performance is likely due to the change made, rather than random chance.

### How it works

- If Variant B has a 20% open rate and Variant A has a 18% open rate, but the sample was only 50 people, the confidence will be low.
- If the same difference is seen with a sample of 2,000 people, the confidence will be very high (99%+).

## 4. Multi-Stage Optimization

For major product launches, we recommend a 3-stage test:

1. **Week 1 (Subject)**: Find the best subject line.
2. **Week 2 (Content)**: Use the winning subject and test two content versions.
3. **Week 3 (CTA)**: Use the winning subject + content and test the final call-to-action.
